The shop

Things you can hold.

Nothing in this world costs money and nothing in it ever will. There is no account to buy, no item to buy, and no way at all to turn money into an advantage over somebody who has none. That is a rule, fixed in the founding along with the rest of them, and not a policy anybody can change their mind about later.

This page sells objects instead. They are here because a thing you can hand to somebody does what a link cannot, and because a book is better on paper. Everything is sold at what it costs to make and post. Nothing is added on top and nothing here funds the world, because the world does not need funding.

The handbook

Twenty pages, A5, saddle stitched. It says what the world is made of: the nine crafts, the seventeen trades you can swear to, why nothing outside your own trade passes seventy, what a pack holds, what a shield costs, what lives here.

Every number in it is generated from the rules rather than written beside them, and the last page carries the founding hash. A world whose rules cannot change is a world a printed book can describe without going out of date.

It does not say where anything is, what to do, or how to get rich. None of that is a rule, so none of it is in the book.

The blank ledger

The handbook refuses to tell you where anything is. This is where you write it down.

A hundred and sixty ruled pages, A5, the same board and cloth as the handbook. The island is printed faint on the endpapers with the ruling over it, so you can mark a place and still write beside it. Nothing else is printed inside. The first page has a line for your citizen's name.

No two of them will say the same thing, and none is worth anything to anybody who has not walked the same ground. That is the whole reason the handbook withholds it: so that this one is yours.

THE LEDGER

The shirt

The island's coastline, printed small on the chest. No name on it, no logo, and nothing that explains itself. It is a shape that means nothing to anybody who has not walked it.

The outline is traced from the world's own geography rather than drawn to look like a map. It is the shore the generator laid down at the founding, read back from the rule that decides where water is, with the rivers and lakes it actually has. Nothing is stylised and nothing is moved to make it a better shape.

THE SHIRT

Stickers

Six to a sheet, the island in silhouette, about fifty millimetres across. The lakes are holes in it and the rivers cut in from the shore, because they do.

This is the cheapest thing here and the one most likely to reach somebody who has never heard of any of it. A shirt is seen by the people in the room. A sticker goes on a laptop and stays there.

THE STICKERS

The print

The same coastline, on paper, large. It is drawn as vector rather than as a picture, so it is the same at any size the printer will run.

There are no names on it and no marks, which makes it the one thing here that is a map and gives nothing away. Whether it is the island or a shape depends entirely on whether you have been there.

THE PRINT

Not open yet. Each of these goes live the moment there is somewhere for it to point.

Why sell anything at all

Because everything else here disappears when the last machine stops. The rules survive that and so does the record, but only for whoever still has a copy. A book on a shelf is a copy somebody keeps without meaning to, and it is the one form of this world that needs no computer to be read.

And because a world nobody can buy their way into is worth saying out loud. A sticker says it to one person at a time, which is slower than an advertisement and lasts longer.

Printed on demand. Nothing is stocked and nothing is warehoused. The printer takes the money, makes the thing and posts it. What reaches this project is nothing, which is the arrangement that keeps the rule at the top of this page true.

The artwork is yours too

The coastline on the shirt, the stickers and the print is a file in the repository, and so is the tool that made it. Run node coastline.mjs against a founding record and you get the same three files: the line, the endpaper and the sticker sheet. They will match, because the geography is a pure function of the genesis and is hashed into it.

So you can take these to your own printer and skip this page. That is not a loophole. It is the same arrangement as the handbook, which has always been free to print, and buying one has always been for people who would rather not.

Print the handbook yourself

It is imposed for saddle stitching already: A4, double sided, landscape, flipping on the short edge, folded in half with two staples in the crease. Ten minutes and some paper. The handbook page has the file and the rest of the instructions.
