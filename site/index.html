PLAY IN YOUR BROWSER
No account or download, and forever ad-free. Nothing to install. It is one web page.

Free forever. No company, no servers, no subscription. The world runs on the computers of the people playing in it, and stays alive as long as anyone cares.

Your key is your character. A cryptographic keypair on your machine. No account, no password reset, no one to confiscate it.

Any window you like. There is no official look. This site's client is one window among many; the same world renders in a terminal, in ASCII, or in whatever anyone builds.

No combat level. A citizen is introduced by their standing, the sum of all sixteen skills, and their calling, the trade they are best at. Combat is three skills of sixteen, and this world does not rank its people by their capacity for violence.

Bots hold the lanterns. An executor runs its own node, like everyone else, so the same script that grinds woodcutting is also verifying every interval and enforcing the constitution on everyone, itself included. Automation is priced into the economy; presence cannot be automated.

What you do here

Interval is a small online world in the spirit of early-2000s browser games. Tallyholm is one island of seven countries — the Greenwood, the Heartlands, the Downs, the Moor, the Crags, the Fens and the Wilds — and nineteen named places within them. Cut timber in the Greenwood, dig the Crags, fish the Fens, fight the skeleton-knights that muster in the Wilds, forge star gear, brew ale in a house of your own, chart the frontier and sell the way, or keep a stall so somebody else does not have to walk. Every 600 milliseconds the world advances one interval, and everything that ever happens, happens on one.

How it works

There is no game server. The rules are written in a public constitution, every node computes the world independently, and a quorum of witnesses certifies each interval — a proof anyone can check against the founding record alone. Under a network split the world stops rather than fork: a stopped world, never two. Nobody can shut it down, charge for it, or change it out from under you. An update you don't like is just a different world you don't have to move to.

The island

The world is an island named Tallyholm, a tally being the split stick whose two halves prove each other, which is how this world stays real. What follows is not a picture of the map: it is the map, and the living copy is computed in your browser from the seed by the same functions every node verifies. Seven towns, two passes through the Ridge, a causeway walked once and recalled to forever, and one island in the northwest that appears on no road and carries no label.
